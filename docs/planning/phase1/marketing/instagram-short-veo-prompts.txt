--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START — three things that decide whether this works
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. DO NOT ask the model for the product UI. Every prompt below deliberately
   keeps the phone screen angled away from the lens. The real interface is cut
   in afterwards as a full-frame screen recording (S1/S2/S3 at the bottom).

2. THE SAME WOMAN MUST APPEAR IN ALL SEVEN. The character wording is repeated
   verbatim in each prompt below — do not paraphrase it between clips. Use the
   last frame of each clip as the reference image for the next.

3. GENERATE 4–6 VARIANTS PER CLIP and expect to keep one. Clip 6 is the one to
   generate most and judge hardest.

Do not put the spoken lines in the prompts. Record or synthesise ONE continuous
voice track over the finished cut — seven separately generated voices drift, and
the joins are what make a video read as AI.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEGATIVE PROMPT — use on all seven clips
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

on-screen text, subtitles, captions, watermarks, logos, user interface elements,
readable screen content, distorted hands, extra fingers, warped faces, plastic
skin, oversaturated colours, slow motion, drone shot, cinematic colour grade,
studio lighting, stock-footage look, crowds staring at camera

================================================================================

a plain white t-shirt, straight-leg indigo jeans, small gold hoop earrings, and
a tan canvas crossbody bag. Natural skin texture with visible pores and fine
lines. Ordinary, believable, not a model.

Bürkliplatz in Zürich, talking directly to the lens with an easy, unforced
expression, one hand gesturing lightly. Behind her, Lake Zürich stretches out
with small moored boats, a passenger ferry in the middle distance, and the faint
silhouette of the Alps on the horizon. A few pedestrians and a cyclist pass
naturally in the background, none of them looking at the camera. The camera
walks backwards ahead of her, keeping her from the waist up, her eyeline just
above lens height.

Shot on a modern smartphone in 4K, vertical 9:16 aspect ratio. Handheld with
slight natural sway and micro-jitter, as if held by a friend walking backwards.
Shallow depth of field, background softly out of focus. Golden hour in Zürich,
low warm sunlight raking across Lake Zürich, water sparkling behind her.
Documentary realism. Natural colour, no cinematic teal-and-orange grade, no lens
flares, no slow motion. Candid social-media footage, not an advertisement.

Ambient audio: light wind, water lapping, distant gulls, footsteps on stone.

================================================================================
THE VOICE TRACK — one continuous take over the finished cut
================================================================================

Third person throughout. She is describing what a maker gets; she is NOT the
maker and must not claim to use the product herself. See §7a and §7b of the
companion doc for why, and for what she may say instead.

  1  If you make things for a living in Switzerland — this is the part nobody
     warns you about.

  2  You're not just making the thing. You're photographing it, writing it up,
     building the website, keeping the stock list, and doing the maths at eleven
     at night.

  3  So this one is interesting. She photographed a page of her notebook — and
     that's the catalogue. Names, prices, quantities. She didn't type any of it.

  4  And at a market, this is the till. She taps the photo of the actual piece —
     then TWINT, card or cash, same screen. That's the bit the card companies
     don't do.

  5  Same piece is already on her website — written up in German, French,
     Italian and English. One stock count across both. Sell it here, it's gone
     there. Instantly.

  6  Is it the cheapest way to take a card? No. It isn't. But that's not what
     she's paying for — she's paying to not spend her evenings doing admin.

  7  Zolto. It's Swiss, and it's free to start.

Delivery: unpolished, one or two natural stumbles left in, no presenter cadence.
Line 6 must sound offhand — an aside, not a line. Swiss-German-accented English
reads local and trustworthy. A German version is worth making from the same
picture cut.


================================================================================
THE THREE SCREEN RECORDINGS — record these from the real app
================================================================================

Capture from a real store with real stock, using the device's own screen
recorder. Never film a screen with a camera.

  S1 · 4s · phone, 9:16 · into clip 3
     Notebook page photographed, then the catalogue filling in — names, prices
     and quantities appearing as rows.

  S2 · 5s · phone, 9:16 · into clip 4
     The POS grid of product photographs, thumb taps one, the TWINT / card /
     cash row appears, TWINT chosen.

  S3 · 4s · laptop + phone composed as 9:16 split · into clip 5
     Storefront listing above, POS below, one stock count dropping on both at
     the same instant.

  - S1 and S2 are already 9:16 and drop in full-frame with no crop.
  - For S3, compose a split rather than shrinking a whole browser window — a
    scaled-down desktop window is illegible again.
  - Record the interactions slowly. Real usage speed is unreadable at Reels
    length; speeding up in the edit is easy, slowing down is not.
  - Do Not Disturb on, full battery, no notification banners.
  - Warm the recordings slightly in the grade so the cutaways don't feel pasted
    into golden-hour footage.


================================================================================
ASSEMBLY
================================================================================

1. Stitch the seven clips in order. Trim 0.5–1s off each end where motion
   settles.
2. Drop in S1/S2/S3 as hard cuts, full frame. The product must be legible for at
   least 2 seconds in each. No dissolves.
3. Lay the voice over the whole cut, then nudge picture to speech.
4. Captions: bold condensed sans, word-by-word pop-on, white with a hard dark
   outline, gold #B8963E on one or two key words. Safe areas in 1080×1920 —
   120px top, 420px bottom, 60px sides.
5. Keep an ambience bed under the voice: lake, wind, gulls, a tram. A clean mix
   is what makes AI footage feel synthetic.
6. Export 1080×1920 H.264. Also cut a 15-second version from clips 1, 4, 6, 7.
7. Apply Instagram's AI-content label.
