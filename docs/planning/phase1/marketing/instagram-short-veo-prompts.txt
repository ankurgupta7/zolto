--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY FIVE AND NOT SEVEN — the arithmetic, so you can check it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The original seven-beat script ran ~60 seconds of speech inside a 45-second cut.
Every beat but the last was over its slot, which is also why the beats couldn't
be merged: beats 1 and 2 alone were ~16 seconds of talking, and no 8-second
generation holds that. The lines below are trimmed to ~48 seconds, which is what
makes five setups possible.

  SETUP        BEATS   TOTAL   FOOTAGE OF HER   SCREEN RECORDING
  A            1 + 2    14s    14s (8s + one extend)      —
  B            3         7s     3s                        S1  4s
  C            4         9s     4s                        S2  5s
  D            5         8s     4s                        S3  4s
  E            6 + 7    11s     8s                        —  (end card, 3s)
                       ----   ----
                        49s    33s of generated footage

Beat 7 gets no generation of its own — it is voiced over the held final frame of
setup E, under the end card. That merge is free.

Setup A is one location and one continuous action, so use the tool's EXTEND /
continuation feature for its second half rather than generating a fresh clip.
Continuity is then handled for you instead of by hand.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BEFORE YOU START — three things that decide whether this works
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. DO NOT ask the model for the product UI. Every prompt below deliberately
   keeps the phone screen angled away from the lens. The real interface is cut
   in afterwards as a full-frame screen recording (S1/S2/S3, at the bottom).

2. THE SAME WOMAN MUST APPEAR IN ALL FIVE. The character wording is repeated
   verbatim in each prompt — do not paraphrase it between setups. Use the last
   frame of each setup as the reference image for the next.

3. GENERATE 4–6 VARIANTS PER SETUP and expect to keep one. Setup E is the one to
   generate most and judge hardest — see the note under it.

Do not put the spoken lines in the prompts. Record or synthesise ONE continuous
voice track over the finished cut — separately generated voices drift, and the
joins are what make a video read as AI.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NEGATIVE PROMPT — use on all five setups
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

on-screen text, subtitles, captions, watermarks, logos, user interface elements,
readable screen content, distorted hands, extra fingers, warped faces, plastic
skin, oversaturated colours, slow motion, drone shot, cinematic colour grade,
studio lighting, stock-footage look, crowds staring at camera

SETUP A · 0–14s · THE HOOK AND THE OTHER HALF OF THE JOB   (beats 1 + 2)
Generate 8s, then EXTEND to ~14s. One continuous walk.
================================================================================

A 32-year-old Swiss woman, warm and relaxed, shoulder-length dark blonde hair
loosely tied back with strands escaping in the wind, light freckles across the
nose, minimal natural makeup. She wears an unbuttoned cream linen overshirt over
a plain white t-shirt, straight-leg indigo jeans, small gold hoop earrings, and
a tan canvas crossbody bag. Natural skin texture with visible pores and fine
lines. Ordinary, believable, not a model.

She walks slowly toward the camera along the wide stone promenade at
Bürkliplatz in Zürich, talking directly to the lens with an easy, unforced
expression. She begins with one hand gesturing lightly, then starts counting
items off on the fingers of her right hand, one at a time, ending with a small
wry shrug. Behind her, Lake Zürich stretches out with small moored boats, a
passenger ferry in the middle distance, and the faint silhouette of the Alps on
the horizon; plane trees line the path on her right. A few pedestrians and a
cyclist pass naturally in the background, none of them looking at the camera.
The camera walks backwards ahead of her throughout, keeping her from the waist
up, her eyeline just above lens height, the distance and height unchanging.

Shot on a modern smartphone in 4K, vertical 9:16 aspect ratio. Handheld with
slight natural sway and micro-jitter, as if held by a friend walking backwards.
Shallow depth of field, background softly out of focus. Golden hour in Zürich,
low warm sunlight raking across Lake Zürich, water sparkling behind her.
Documentary realism. Natural colour, no cinematic teal-and-orange grade, no lens
flares, no slow motion. Candid social-media footage, not an advertisement.

Ambient audio: light wind, water lapping, distant gulls, footsteps on stone, a
distant tram bell.

>> The finger-count should land in the extended half. If the extend loses her
   face or changes the walk, re-roll the extend rather than the whole setup.

================================================================================
THE VOICE TRACK — one continuous take over the finished cut
================================================================================

Trimmed to ~48s. The originals ran ~60s of speech against a 45s cut, which is
what forced seven setups. Third person throughout: she is describing what a
maker gets. She is NOT the maker, must not claim to use the product herself, and
must not attribute opinions to unnamed others — see §7a and §7b of the companion
doc for why, and for what she may say instead.

  A/1  If you make things for a living in Switzerland — nobody warns you about
       this part.

  A/2  You're not just making the thing. You're photographing it, writing it up,
       building the website, doing the maths at eleven at night.

  B/3  She photographed a page of her notebook. That's the catalogue — names,
       prices, quantities. She didn't type any of it.

  C/4  At a market, this is the till. She taps the actual piece — then TWINT,
       card or cash, same screen. Card companies don't do that.

  D/5  It's already on her website, written up in four languages. One stock
       count across both — sell it here, it's gone there.

  E/6  Is it the cheapest way to take a card? No. It isn't. She's paying to not
       spend her evenings on admin.

  E/7  Gwinn. Swiss, and free to start.

Delivery: unpolished, one or two natural stumbles left in, no presenter cadence.
Line E/6 must sound offhand — an aside, not a line. Swiss-German-accented
English reads local and trustworthy. A German version is worth making from the
same picture cut.

Captions carry what the trim removed: name the four languages on screen under
D/5, and put "not the cheapest. that's not the point." under E/6.


================================================================================
THE THREE SCREEN RECORDINGS — record these from the real app
================================================================================

Capture from a real store with real stock, using the device's own screen
recorder. Never film a screen with a camera.

  S1 · 4s · phone, 9:16 · into setup B
     Notebook page photographed, then the catalogue filling in — names, prices
     and quantities appearing as rows.

  S2 · 5s · phone, 9:16 · into setup C
     The POS grid of product photographs, thumb taps one, the TWINT / card /
     cash row appears, TWINT chosen.

  S3 · 4s · laptop + phone composed as a 9:16 split · into setup D
     Storefront listing above, POS below, one stock count dropping on both at
     the same instant.

  - S1 and S2 are already 9:16 and drop in full-frame with no crop.
  - For S3, compose a split rather than shrinking a whole browser window — a
    scaled-down desktop window is illegible again, which is the problem we
    started with.
  - Record the interactions slowly. Real usage speed is unreadable at Reels
    length; speeding up in the edit is easy, slowing down is not.
  - Do Not Disturb on, full battery, no notification banners.
  - Warm the recordings slightly in the grade so the cutaways don't feel pasted
    into golden-hour footage.


================================================================================
ASSEMBLY
================================================================================

1. Generate SETUP E first and judge it. Everything else is wasted if the
   concession sounds performed.
2. Stitch A, B, C, D, E in order. Trim 0.5–1s off each end where motion settles.
3. Drop in S1/S2/S3 as hard cuts, full frame, per the EDIT notes. The product
   must be legible for at least 2 seconds in each. No dissolves.
4. Hold E's final frame ~3s under the end card.
5. Lay the voice over the whole cut, then nudge picture to speech.
6. Captions: bold condensed sans, word-by-word pop-on, white with a hard dark
   outline, gold #B8963E on one or two key words. Safe areas in 1080×1920 —
   120px top, 420px bottom, 60px sides.
7. Keep an ambience bed under the voice: lake, wind, gulls, a tram. A clean mix
   is what makes AI footage feel synthetic.
8. Export 1080×1920 H.264. Also cut a 15-second version from A, C and E.
9. Apply Instagram's AI-content label.
