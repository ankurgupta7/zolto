# Launch Diary #2: Going Live

> How Sheena Arora went from Christmas markets to her first online store —  
> **Part 2: The Launch**

---

Yesterday, Sheena configured Kalakosh's store. Today, she went live.

Not with a marketing campaign. Not with a launch party. With a single Instagram story: "We finally have a website. Link in bio."

This is what actually happens when a maker launches online. No viral moment. Just a quiet switch from "not available" to "here it is."

---

## The Moment

The store went live at 10:00 AM. Sheena posted an Instagram story showing the new website on her phone, with a caption that said: "Took me three days, but here it is. You can now buy Kalakosh online."

No discount code. No "launch special." Just the store, live.

Within an hour, the first visitor arrived. Not from an ad. From the Instagram story. They browsed three pearl necklaces, added one to cart, and... closed the tab.

**First lesson:** Most visitors don't buy on the first visit. That's normal. The store being live is step one. Trust-building is step two.

## The Traffic (Day 1)

| Source | Visitors | Orders | Notes |
|--------|----------|--------|-------|
| Instagram (story + bio link) | 23 | 0 | Existing customers curious |
| WhatsApp (direct shares) | 8 | 0 | Friends and family |
| Direct (typed URL) | 3 | 0 | Probably Sheena testing |
| **Total** | **34** | **0** | **Day 1 is about presence, not sales** |

Zero orders on Day 1. This is not a failure. It's reality. A new store with no SEO history, no ads, and a small Instagram following gets visitors, not conversions. The job of Day 1 is to exist.

## What Worked

1. **The Instagram story got the most traffic.** Sheena's existing audience (built at Christmas markets and Chilbis along the Gold Coast) is on Instagram. That's where the announcement belongs.
2. **The product photos mattered.** Visitors who clicked through spent an average of 2 minutes on product pages. The photos — taken with a phone on a white background — were good enough.
3. **The "About" page got unexpected traffic.** 40% of visitors clicked "About" before looking at products. People want to know who they're buying from. They wanted to know who Sheena is, how she started at markets, why she works with pearls.

## What Didn't

1. **No one used the AI chatbot.** It was visible but uninvited. Visitors browsed silently. This is expected — chatbots get used when people have questions, not when they're just looking.
2. **The mobile experience had a glitch.** On some Android phones, the product grid was slightly misaligned. Fixed by Day 2.
3. **Shipping wasn't clear enough.** Two visitors added to cart but didn't check out. Post-launch fix: added "Shipping: CHF 8 Switzerland, CHF 15 EU" below the "Add to Cart" button.

## The Fix Cycle

This is where the AI-run model shows its value. Sheena noticed the shipping clarity issue and mentioned it to the AI chatbot: "People aren't checking out. I think they don't know the shipping cost."

The chatbot suggested adding the shipping cost to the product page. Sheena approved. The change was deployed to the store in 10 minutes.

No ticket. No email. No "we'll add it to the backlog." Just: problem → suggestion → fix → deployed.

## Day 2: The First Order

At 9:47 AM on Day 2, the first order came in. A freshwater pearl necklace, CHF 65 + CHF 8 shipping. The customer had met Sheena at a Christmas market three weeks ago, lost the business card, and remembered the Instagram handle.

This is exactly why the store exists. Not for impulse purchases from strangers. For the person who met Sheena once, wanted to buy later, and finally has a way to do it.

**Order details:**
- Product: Freshwater pearl necklace with silver clasp
- Price: CHF 65
- Shipping: CHF 8
- Customer: Found via Instagram, previous market visitor
- Time from store launch to first order: 23 hours, 47 minutes

## What We Learned

1. **Launch without expectations.** Day 1 traffic is curiosity, not conversion. Day 2+ is where sales happen.
2. **Existing audience converts first.** The first customer already knew Sheena and Kalakosh. Online sales start with people who met you offline.
3. **Small fixes matter.** Adding shipping cost to the product page probably saved 2–3 abandoned carts.
4. **The AI chatbot is a feature builder, not just support.** The shipping fix came from a conversation, not a bug report.

## What's Next

The store is live. The first order proved the model works. Now: documenting the process, optimizing the experience, and preparing for organic discovery (SEO, content).
